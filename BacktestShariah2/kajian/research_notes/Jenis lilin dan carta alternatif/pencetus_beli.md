## 1. What do rigorous studies find (with numbers)?

### Takeaway
On large-cap US stocks, the rigorous studies agree. Classic Nison/Morris candlestick patterns used as fixed-holding entry signals show no reliable predictive power and no profit after costs (Marshall-Young-Rose 2006; Horton 2009; Duvinage et al. 2013 after SPA). Positive results cluster in a few places: emerging or Asian markets (Taiwan, China), small or illiquid stocks, older samples (Caginalp & Laurent 1992–96), and papers that change the exit rule or add a trend filter (Lu et al.). Those papers also tested many variants, so they are the most exposed to data snooping. Bulkowski's "reversal rates" for the popular bullish patterns are 57–63%, which he himself calls close to random.

### Cited Findings
**US studies**
- **Marshall, Young & Rose (2006), J. Banking & Finance 30(8):2303–2323.** Tested 28 candlestick signals on DJIA stocks, 1992–2002. Positions were opened at the close on the day after the signal and held 10 days, and significance was set by bootstrap. They found no profit and concluded that candlestick patterns "have no forecasting power" on DJIA stocks. **[US-2000+ partly]** — [IDEAS/RePEc listing via search](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v61y2015icp172-183.html); [Lund thesis literature review summarising MYR](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download?func=downloadFile&recordOId=8877738&fileOId=8877838); [Tharavanij et al. 2017 review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244017736799)
- Short follow-up, Marshall, Young & Rose (2007), "Market timing with candlestick technical analysis," J. Financial Transformation 20:18–25. It concludes that candlestick strategies "are not generally profitable when applied to large U.S. stocks." — [Massey/MQL5 PDF listing](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/211/Market%20Timing%20with%20Candlestick%20Technical%20Analysis.pdf)
- A Japan version by Marshall, Young & Cahan (2008), Review of Quantitative Finance & Accounting 31(2):191–207, covering 1975–2004, also found candlestick strategies "not profitable in the Japanese equity market." **[non-US]** — [RePEc listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/rqfnac/v31y2008i2p191-207.html); [Lund thesis summary](https://lup.lub.lu.se/luur/download?func=downloadFile&recordOId=8877738&fileOId=8877838)
- **Horton (2009), "Stars, crows, and doji: The use of candlesticks in stock selection," Quarterly Review of Economics & Finance 49(2):283–294.** Covered 349 stocks; sources disagree on the universe ("from Commodity Systems Inc." vs. "listed in the S&P 500"). Patterns included Three White Soldiers, Three Black Crows, Morning/Evening Star and doji. He found "little value in the use of candlesticks," which supports weak-form efficiency and contradicts the earlier Caginalp–Laurent result. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223139330_Stars_crows_and_doji_The_use_of_candlesticks_in_stock_selection); [FHSU JIIBR review](https://scholars.fhsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1101&context=jiibr)
- **Caginalp & Laurent (1998), "The predictive power of price patterns," Applied Mathematical Finance 5:181–205.** Tested 8 three-day reversal patterns on S&P 500 stocks, 1992–1996, using Z and binomial tests. One summary reports statistical significance and "almost 1% return over a 2-day horizon" (secondary). The paper's famous headline is "profits compounding at an annual rate as high as 200%" after costs, a figure the author of the summarising source treats sceptically. **[US-old]** — [Goo et al. 2007 review](https://businessperspectives.org/images/pdf/applications/publishing/templates/article/assets/1934/imfi_en_2007_04_1_Goo.pdf); [Tharavanij et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2158244017736799)
- A later DJIA study reports that "eight reversal three-day patterns measured by the Caginalp–Laurent holding strategy appear to be profitable after… transaction costs at 0.5%" (secondary; author and year not confirmed in snippets). — [search summary citing IJEFI PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/2e3a/777a2b811702126a619ee3856a7c1802769b.pdf)
- **Duvinage, Mazza & Petitjean (2013), Quantitative Finance.** Used 5-minute candles on the 30 DJIA stocks, with a bootstrap that simulates OHLC. About a third of the rules beat buy-and-hold at the conservative Bonferroni level before costs. After costs only a few remain, and after the SSPA data-snooping correction "no single candlestick rule beats the buy-and-hold strategy" once costs are counted. Fully automated systems built from the best rules also failed after costs. **[US-2000+, intraday]** — [SSRN 2125889](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2125889); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256029886_The_Intraday_Performance_of_Market_Timing_Strategies_and_Trading_Systems_Based_on_Japanese_Candlesticks). *Conflict:* Tharavanij et al. (2017) describe a DJIA 1992–2012 study as finding candles with a "CL exit strategy are profitable even after transaction costs and corrections of data snooping bias." This appears to be a different paper (likely Lu & Shiu) or a misattribution. Trust the Duvinage abstract. — [Tharavanij 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2158244017736799)
- **Lu & Shiu (2016), "Can 1-day candlestick patterns be profitable on the 30 component stocks of the DJIA?", Applied Economics.** Tested every possible 1-day pattern. A secondary source reports "some patterns still proving profitable especially since 1992" and a "noticeable increase in the predictive power of 1-day patterns from 1992 onwards" (secondary; DJIA 1974–2009 data). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291557612_Can_1-day_candlestick_patterns_be_profitable_on_the_30_component_stocks_of_the_DJIA)
- **Lo, Mamaysky & Wang (2000), J. Finance.** Applied kernel-regression detection of chart patterns (head-and-shoulders, double bottoms and others; not candlesticks) to US stocks, 1962–1996. Conditional return distributions differ from unconditional ones, so "several technical indicators do provide incremental information," especially for **Nasdaq** (smaller) stocks. They also note that statistically informative patterns need not be profitable. **[US-old]** — [NBER w7613](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613); [MIT page](https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/techanal.html)

**Bulkowski and practitioner statistics**
- **Bullish engulfing:** bullish reversal **63%** of the time, reversal rank 22 of 103, but **overall performance rank 84 of 103**. — [thepatternsite BullEngulfing](https://www.thepatternsite.com/BullEngulfing.html)
- **Hammer:** acts as a bullish reversal **60%** of the time (reversal rank 26), overall performance rank 65; Bulkowski notes it is "not far from random (50%)." — [thepatternsite Hammer](https://thepatternsite.com/Hammer.html)
- **Bearish three-line strike** (three black candles, then a white candle engulfing all three): acts as a **bullish reversal 84%** of the time and ranks first overall, but its frequency rank is 94 of 103, so it is very rare. **Upside tasuki gap:** bullish continuation 57% ("near random"), overall rank 5. — [thepatternsite ThreeLineStrikeBear](https://thepatternsite.com/ThreeLineStrikeBear.html); [UpsideTasukiGap](https://thepatternsite.com/UpsideTasukiGap.html); [Top 10 performers](https://thepatternsite.com/CandlePerformers.html)
- Caveat on Bulkowski's method: his "reversal rate" measures the direction of the breakout from the pattern's high or low, not a return from next-day open with a fixed hold. Performance figures are averages without significance tests or costs. Treat them as descriptive. **[blog/practitioner]**
- **Read the Tape (independent, 153 S&P 500 stocks, 2005–2025):** after a bullish engulfing, 56.0% of 8,426 instances closed higher 5 sessions later, against a 54.7% base rate. That is an edge of about 1.3 percentage points. **[US-2000+, blog]** — [readthetape.cc](https://readthetape.cc/tested/bullish-engulfing)
- **QuantifiedStrategies** tested 75 patterns on **SPY**, not single stocks, 1993 onward, buying at the close and exiting when the close is above the prior high. Bearish engulfing (bought as a mean-reversion long) beat bullish engulfing, and "Hanging Man" and "Hammer" ranked as the best single candles. Its signals behave as short-term (1–5 day) mean reversion on an index. **[blog, index]** — [QS engulfing](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/bullish-engulfing-candlestick-pattern-analysis/); [QS single candles](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/single-candlestick-patterns-backtest/); [QS 75 patterns](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/types-candlestick-patterns/)

**Machine learning on candles and charts**
- **Jiang, Kelly & Xiu (2023), "(Re-)Imag(in)ing Price Trends," J. Finance 78(6):3193–3249.** A CNN trained on 5/20/60-day OHLC + MA + volume images of US stocks predicts future returns. The patterns transfer to longer horizons and to international markets. A replication README reports directional accuracy of about 53% and long-short decile Sharpe ratios of about 7.2 weekly and 2.4 monthly (secondary; gross of costs, long-short, all CRSP including micro-caps). **[US-2000+ partly; most credible positive "chart" evidence]** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13268); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3756587); [replication README](https://github.com/Reed2006/-Re-imaging-the-price-trend)
- **CNN on chart images with YOLOv8-detected candle patterns (arXiv 2501.12239, 2025):** adding explicit candlestick patterns **did not improve** on the image-only model. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.12239)
- **Kusuma et al. (2019, arXiv 1903.12258):** CNN classification accuracy of about 92% on Taiwan and Indonesian candle images. This is a direction-classification accuracy with likely leakage and overlapping windows, not a tradable return. Low credibility. **[non-US]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.12258)
- A 2026 benchmark asks whether vision-language models "truly read" candlesticks (results not retrieved). — [arXiv 2604.12659](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12659)

### Inferences
- The US large-cap evidence (MYR 2006, Horton 2009, Duvinage 2013 after SPA, Read the Tape 2005–2025) is consistent with our 17 Oct 2026 finding: 47 catalogue bar patterns, 0 adopted, results about random. A small edge of 1–2 percentage points in hit rate exists at best. That is far below the cost and noise needed to reach a target of at least +10% in 1–4 weeks.
- The positive findings come from studies that (a) changed the exit rule (pattern-to-opposite-pattern holding, or CL exits), (b) added a trend filter, (c) used less efficient markets or small, illiquid stocks, or (d) tested hundreds of variants. Under (a) and (b), the edge probably comes from the trend or exit component, not from the candle shape.
- Jiang-Kelly-Xiu suggests that information in OHLC images exists, but it is spread across 5–60 bars and learned non-linearly, mostly short-horizon reversal and momentum. Hand-named one- to three-bar patterns capture little of it; the 2025 YOLO paper is consistent with this.

### Gaps
- Exact MYR (2006) tables (mean 10-day returns per pattern, bootstrap p-values) could not be read because the fetch failed.
- Morris (Candlestick Charting Explained) statistics and Bulkowski's per-pattern 10-day move percentages and condition tables were not retrieved.
- Results of Lu et al. (2015, JBF) were not retrieved, nor whether they tested US data.
- No 2020–2026 peer-reviewed study of hand-defined candles on the broad US cross-section (including small caps) was found.

## 2. Continuation vs. reversal, trend context, volume, volatility, regime, size; P&F and other chart types

### Takeaway
The conditions with the most support are **trend context** (Lu 2014: profitable only "with the correct trend") and **firm characteristics**: small, highly liquid stocks for bullish reversals in China (Zhu-Atri-Yegen 2016); Nasdaq or smaller stocks in Lo-Mamaysky-Wang. Volume confirmation is barely tested in the academic candle literature. Point & Figure double-top breakouts showed **no edge** in Fidelity's 1990–2006 US test. Rigorous studies of Heikin-Ashi, Renko, Kagi or Three Line Break as stock entry signals are essentially absent; the available evidence is from blogs and is weak or negative.

### Cited Findings
- **Trend filter:** Lu (2014, Taiwan) found 1-day candles "with the correct trend" profitable after costs. **[non-US]** — [Tharavanij 2017 review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2158244017736799). The JBF 2015 title, "Trend definition or holding strategy: What determines the profitability…", shows the authors treat trend definition as a main driver. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378426615002678)
- **Size and liquidity:** Zhu, Atri & Yegen (2016), Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 37:116–127, China 1999–2008, 10 reversal patterns. Bullish harami, engulfing and piercing were profitable for **highly liquid, small** stocks, even in bearish markets. Bearish harami and cross worked for low-liquidity stocks. No volatility split was reported. **[non-US, pre-2009]** — [SSRN 2862775](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2862775); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X15300172)
- **Lo-Mamaysky-Wang:** incremental information was strongest in Nasdaq stocks. **[US-old]** — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613)
- **Horizon:** in China (Chen et al. 2016), accuracy decays as the horizon lengthens — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016PhyA..457..148C/abstract). The Taiwan review finds the best holding period for reversal patterns at about 10–15 days (secondary) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259136529_The_profitability_of_candlestick_charting_in_the_Taiwan_stock_market).
- **Volume:** Fock et al. (2005) did not test volume and suggested it for future research. A later study added volume change, next-day open and real-body gap as confirmation factors (results not retrieved). A Turkish paper, "Candlestick Charting and Trading Volume," exists (results not retrieved). — [FHSU review](https://scholars.fhsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1101&context=jiibr); [Pinpoint and synergistic strategies (CCSE)](https://www.ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/ijef/article/download/5467/6589); [DergiPark](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/367478)
- **Continuation vs. reversal (Bulkowski):** the bullish continuation candle he tests (upside tasuki gap) breaks upward only 57% of the time. The best-ranked "bullish" signal is the bearish three-line strike, which really works as a **reversal after three down days**, i.e. short-term mean reversion. — [UpsideTasukiGap](https://thepatternsite.com/UpsideTasukiGap.html); [ThreeLineStrikeBear](https://thepatternsite.com/ThreeLineStrikeBear.html)
- **QuantifiedStrategies (SPY):** bearish-looking candles bought as longs (bearish engulfing, hanging man) beat bullish ones. This is evidence that on a large-cap index the dominant daily effect is short-term reversal, not the pattern's "meaning." **[blog, index]** — [QS engulfing](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/bullish-engulfing-candlestick-pattern-analysis/)
- **Point & Figure (Fidelity "Testing Point & Figure Patterns," US stocks 1990–2006):**
  - Double-top buy signal: 5-day win rate about 49% with average −0.1%, against the market's about 56% and +0.2%.
  - At 60 days: +2.2% against +2.3% for the market.
  - Conclusion: "relying solely on simple P&F signals does not help traders gain an edge." Only the triple-top signal beat the market at longer horizons.
  **[US-old/2000s]** — [Fidelity PDF](https://www.fidelity.com/bin-public/060_www_fidelity_com/documents/TestingPointFigure.pdf)
- John Anderson's P&F study (cited by AIQ) claims high profitability for P&F patterns, but no design or numbers are available, so it is unverified. — [AIQ](https://aiqeducation.com/mastering-point-and-figure-charts-triple-tops-and-bottoms-in-stocks-and-etfs/)
- **Heikin-Ashi:**
  - QuantifiedStrategies S&P 500 monthly HA trend test since 1960: 85 trades, CAGR 5.2% against 7.5% buy-and-hold, MDD 29% against 52.6%. Lower risk but lower return. **[blog, index]** — [QS substack](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/heikin-ashi-trading-rules-and-strategy); [QS](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/heikin-ashi-trading-strategy/)
  - BJF Trading warns that naive HA backtests fill at synthetic HA prices "the market never offered," which turns losers into "spectacular" winners. Fills must use real next prices. — [BJF](https://bjftradinggroup.com/heikin-ashi-trading-strategy/)
  - Bitcoin HA test: about the same as buy-and-hold. **[crypto]** — [Boring Edge](https://boringedge.com/bitcoin-heikin-ashi-strategy-backtest/)
  - Liberated Stock Trader claims HA "outperforms 66% of equities" without a methodology. **[marketing]** — [LST](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/heikin-ashi-candles/)
- **Renko, Kagi, Three Line Break:** no stock-specific quantified results were found; one comparison page says Renko "depends on how you use them." — [StockChartPro](https://www.stockchartpro.com/stock-charts-for-traders/)

### Inferences
- Our rejected HA Trend entry (K381: CAGR 18.7%, MDD −40.5%, average trade +0.17%) matches the external picture. HA colour changes are lagged, smoothed momentum. They add a trend-following timing layer with modest edge but bring no new information beyond close > close[n], which the rule already used. The QS index result (lower return, lower MDD) is the best case.
- A P&F double-top buy is a box-scaled breakout above a prior high, essentially a Donchian/N-day high breakout. Fidelity's null result for the simple version suggests any value comes from the breakout and momentum context (e.g. high RS), which our other methods already capture.
- If candles add value at all in our setting, the evidence points to two places: (i) a **short-term reversal** trigger (several down days or a bearish candle, then a strong bullish reversal bar) inside a **longer uptrend**, in liquid but smaller stocks; or (ii) a filter or timing tool on top of a method that already has an edge (trend, RS, breakout). It is unlikely to work as a standalone entry.

### Gaps
- No rigorous US study of volume-confirmed candles was found (e.g. engulfing with volume above 1.5× the 20-day average).
- No US study split by volatility or regime (VIX, market above or below its 200-day average) was found.
- No academic evidence was found for Kagi shoulder/waist or Three Line Break reversals. Dorsey's P&F "high pole" or bullish-percent claims are unverified.

## 3. Data snooping / multiple-testing caveats

### Takeaway
The only candle study found that applies a formal data-snooping correction (SSPA, Duvinage et al. 2013) shows that every apparently significant rule disappears after costs. Broader technical-rule studies using White's Reality Check or Hansen's SPA usually overturn developed-market "winners" and sometimes keep emerging-market ones. Most positive candle papers (Taiwan, China, Caginalp-Laurent) test dozens of patterns × horizons × exits without a family-wise correction.

### Cited Findings
- **Duvinage et al. (2013):**
  - Bonferroni level, before costs: about one third of rules beat buy-and-hold.
  - After costs plus the SSPA test: none do.
  — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2125889)
- White's Reality Check (2000) and Hansen's SPA (2005) test whether the best of many rules truly beats a benchmark. SPA reduces the "least favourable configuration" conservatism of the Reality Check and so has more power. — [ResearchGate (Re-examining TA with RC/SPA)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256066609_Re-Examining_the_Profitability_of_Technical_Analysis_with_White's_Reality_Check_and_Hansen's_SPA_Test)
- **FTSE 100 test of 26,410 technical rules:** no significant outperformance of buy-and-hold after data-snooping control. A stepwise SPA (Hsu, Hsu & Kuan 2010) finds significant predictive ability in **emerging** markets. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256066609_Re-Examining_the_Profitability_of_Technical_Analysis_with_White's_Reality_Check_and_Hansen's_SPA_Test); [Hsu-Hsu-Kuan Step-SPA](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~ckuan/pdf/Step-SPA-20090720.pdf); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927539810000022)
- Park & Irwin's review: Reality Check studies are "a substantial improvement" because they quantify the snooping bias. — [Park & Irwin 2004](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/marketing/agmas/AgMAS04_04.pdf)
- The Duvinage abstract notes that significance from closing prices can be overstated by non-synchronous prices. Most candle studies also assume execution at the close of the signal day. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256029886_The_Intraday_Performance_of_Market_Timing_Strategies_and_Trading_Systems_Based_on_Japanese_Candlesticks); [search summary on close execution](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2158244017736799)

### Inferences
- Our own test of 47 catalogue methods with 0 adopted is itself a multiple-testing exercise, and a correct null result is the expected outcome. Any new candle × condition grid should be judged by deflated Sharpe, SPA or a hold-out (e.g. S1–S4, WFO, AUTOTUNE 2000), not by its best in-sample cell.
- Studies that execute at the signal-day close overstate results relative to our rule (signal at close, buy next open). Reversal-bar edges are concentrated in the overnight gap, so they shrink most under next-open entry.

### Gaps
- No published study was found that applies the Reality Check or SPA to daily candlestick patterns across US single stocks. A GitHub research issue asking exactly this question was found, but it is not a result: [GitHub issue](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/1944).

## 4. Most defensible mechanical definitions to test (long-only, US, daily, ≥ +10% in 1–4 weeks)

### Takeaway
No candlestick or chart-type definition has robust, out-of-sample, after-cost US evidence of reaching +10% in 1–4 weeks. The few patterns with any repeatable support are listed below; test them only as **conditional filters** on a trend, RS or breakout method, with formal multiple-testing control.
- Bullish reversal after a short pullback inside an uptrend: piercing, bullish engulfing, bullish harami, or "three-line-strike-like" (3 down closes then a bar engulfing them).
- Restricted to liquid, smaller-cap names.
- Exit by rule or trailing stop, not fixed time.

### Cited Findings
- **Piercing** (Taiwan; significant with bootstrap and OOS under pattern-to-opposite-pattern holding). **[non-US]** — [Lu-Shiu-Liu 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1016/j.rfe.2012.02.001)
- **Bullish harami, engulfing, piercing** in small, highly liquid stocks, including in bear markets. **[non-US]** — [Zhu-Atri-Yegen 2016](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2862775)
- **1-day candles plus correct trend definition**, after costs. **[non-US]** — [Lu 2014 via review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/2158244017736799)
- **Bearish three-line strike** (bullish reversal 84%, rank 1, rare). **[practitioner]** — [Bulkowski](https://thepatternsite.com/ThreeLineStrikeBear.html)
- **Short-term mean reversion longs** on large caps (bearish engulfing or hanging man bought, exit when close > prior high) have a 1–5 day horizon and small per-trade gains. That horizon does not suit a +10% target. **[blog, index]** — [QS](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/bullish-engulfing-candlestick-pattern-analysis/)
- **CNN chart features** (Jiang-Kelly-Xiu) are the only strong evidence on US data. They are a learned signal, not a hand-made pattern, and come from decile long-short portfolios. — [J. Finance 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13268)

### Inferences (proposed test definitions; my own synthesis, not from a source)
- **E1 Pullback-reversal in uptrend:**
  - Trend: close > SMA50 > SMA200, and RS rating ≥ 80.
  - Pullback: at least 3 lower closes, or close ≤ the lower 20-day Bollinger band, or RSI2 < 10.
  - Trigger day: a bullish engulfing (open ≤ prior close, close ≥ prior open, prior bar red; real body ≥ 0.6 × ATR14) or a piercing bar (open < prior low, close > midpoint of the prior red body).
  - Volume on the trigger day ≥ 1.5 × the 20-day median.
  - Universe: liquid (median dollar volume ≥ $5M) and small to mid-cap.
  - Buy next open; exit by method rule or the lane-2 Chandelier.
  - Compare against the identical trend + pullback filter **without** the candle: the candle's marginal value is the quantity being tested.
- **E2 Three-line-strike long:** 3 consecutive lower closes, then a bar whose close exceeds the open of the first of the three, within the E1 trend filter.
- **E3 Do not pursue as standalone triggers:** HA colour change, Renko brick reversal, P&F double-top, Kagi and Three Line Break. The evidence is null or absent, and these signals reduce to moving-average or breakout momentum that other methods already capture.
- Realistic expectation from the literature: any candle filter changes the hit rate by a few percentage points, not the payoff distribution. A +10% target depends on the trend, RS and volatility of the underlying method, not on the trigger bar.

### Gaps
- No source gives the exact thresholds above. They are the author's proposals for testing, consistent with the standard definitions of Nison and Bulkowski.
- No out-of-sample evidence was found on candle patterns with ≥ +10% 1–4 week targets, and no US small-cap candle study after 2010.
